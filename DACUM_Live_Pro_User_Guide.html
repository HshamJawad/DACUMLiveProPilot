🗺 Overview

What is DACUM Live Pro?

DACUM Live Pro is a browser-based occupational analysis platform that walks curriculum developers and TVET specialists through the whole DACUM process — from duties and tasks all the way to structured training modules — with AI assistance at every stage, live workshop voting, and a full interface in English, French and Arabic.

✨
Full Draft generatorOne run builds the whole chain — duties, tasks, supporting information, clusters, criteria, outcomes and modules.
🤖
AI in every tabGeneration cards now sit in Duties & Tasks, Additional Info, Clusters, Learning Outcomes and Module Mapping.
🌐
Three languages, full RTLEnglish, French and Arabic across the interface, the exports and this guide.
📡
Live workshop votingShare a link or QR code; participants rate tasks from any browser and results aggregate instantly.
📁
Multi-project sidebarRun several occupational analyses side by side and switch between them with full state preserved.
📄
PDF & Word exportFormatted DACUM charts and verification reports — with correctly shaped Arabic in both formats.
📴
Works offlineInstallable as an app. Every tab works without a connection — only AI generation and live voting need the network.
💾
JSON save / importSave projects as portable JSON files. Import one or many — each becomes a project in the sidebar.
ℹ️
DACUM Live Pro runs entirely in the browser — nothing to install. Your data lives in the browser’s local storage and in the JSON files you download; it is never uploaded anywhere. Two features do reach the network: AI generation and live workshop voting.

✨ Full Draft

Generate Full DraftNew

DACUM is a chain: every stage is built from the one before it. The Full Draft generator walks that chain in a single run and stops wherever you tell it to. You will find it on the Chart Info tab, directly under the fields it needs.

✨

Running a full draftSix stages, one run

1

Fill in Occupation Title and Scope of Work on the Chart Info tab. Both are required here — every stage inherits the boundary they set, so a gap in them compounds through six stages instead of one.

2

Click ✨ Generate Full Draft to open the generator window.

3

Choose how far down the chain to go — you can stop at duties and tasks, or continue all the way to training modules.

4

Tick the optional extras — Supporting Information is not required by any later stage, so it is safe to skip.

5

Review the notice showing how many of your daily generations the run will use, then click Start Generating.

6

Stages complete one after another. You can press Stop at any point — everything already generated is kept. If a stage fails, everything before it is saved and you can retry from that stage.

7

When it finishes, open each tab and review. Every tab carries a 🔁 Regenerate from here button for when you change something upstream.

⚖️
Task Verification is not generated. Importance, frequency and difficulty are the judgement of a panel of practitioners; their value comes from who produced them. Generated numbers would look identical on the chart and carry none of that authority — so the generator leaves that stage to your panel.
🛡

Draft ratings for preparationOptional — and never silently promoted

If you want provisional ratings to prepare the workshop, the generator can produce them as an explicit option. They arrive tagged unverified: a banner sits above them, they are excluded from PDF and Word exports and from the verification report, and they are never counted as panel data.

Run the workshop, correct them with the panel, then click Accept as verified. Only then do they enter exports — and after that nothing distinguishes them from collected data, which is exactly why the tool asks you to confirm.

🔒
The interface language is locked while a full draft is running. Switching mid-run would leave the early stages in one language and the rest in another.

3

📋

Table viewClassic list editing

Switch with the 📋 Table View button below the AI card. The same data appears as labelled input rows — better for pasting from a spreadsheet or editing in bulk.

📝
Task writing format: action verb + object + context (when it matters).
Calibrate testing equipment according to manufacturer specifications.
Use concrete occupational verbs — install, inspect, calibrate, diagnose — and avoid cognitive ones such as learn, understand or know.
🤖 AI Assistance

AI cards across the toolNew

Generation is no longer confined to Duties & Tasks. Five tabs now carry a purple generation card, each tuned to what that stage actually needs — and each stating plainly what it replaces and what it leaves alone.

Tab What it generates Effect on existing content
Duties & TasksA full set of duties with their tasks, from the occupation details. Adding a scope of work and job title sharpens the result.Replaces existing duties and tasks — you are warned first.
Additional InfoKnowledge, skills, worker behaviours, tools and equipment, future trends, acronyms and career path.Custom sections you added yourself are never overwritten.
Competency ClustersTwo separate steps: first grouping tasks across duties by shared purpose, process or skill; then writing the range and the observable, measurable criteria.Criteria are written for whichever clusters exist — including ones you built by hand.
Learning OutcomesOutcomes from performance criteria, using one of three design patterns — one-to-one, many-to-one, or hybrid.New outcomes are added; existing ones are never replaced.
Module MappingEither one module per outcome — instant and fully offline — or an AI grouping that names each module and sequences them foundational to advanced.Both replace existing modules; your learning outcomes are never changed.
🎯
Scoped, not genericOutput is bounded by the occupation, scope and job title you entered — not by the occupation in the abstract.
↩
Undo covers itGeneration is pushed onto the undo stack — Ctrl+Z reverts it like any other edit.
🔢
Daily allowanceGenerations are metered per day. The Full Draft window tells you how many a run will use before you start it.
📸
Snapshot firstSave a snapshot before any generation that replaces content — it is the cleanest way back.
⚠️
Generated content is a starting point, not a finding. Every stage should be read, corrected and owned by the panel before it goes into a curriculum document.

🎯 Verification

Task Verification tab

Validate the chart through structured rating — either individual expert ratings or live workshop voting with a full panel.

⚖️

Rating dimensionsThree criteria, scale 0–3

Dimension What it measures Scale
ImportanceHow critical is this task to competent job performance?0 = not important → 3 = critical
FrequencyHow often is it performed?0 = rarely → 3 = daily
DifficultyHow hard is it to learn?0 = easy → 3 = very difficult
📊

Priority index and dashboardCalculated automatically from the mean ratings

🔢
I × FPriority index = mean importance × mean frequency. The standard DACUM formula.
🔢
I × F × DAdds mean difficulty — use it when you need training load for sequencing.
📋
Task dashboardAll rated tasks ranked by priority index, with the high-priority band highlighted.
📋
Duty-level summaryAggregated priority and training load per duty, sortable by either.
📂
The dashboard has a project selector: you can read the verification results of any other saved project without leaving the one you are working in.
📡 Live Workshop

Live workshop voting

Collect ratings from the whole panel at once through a shared link or QR code. Participants need nothing but a browser.

🚀

Starting a sessionThree steps

1

With duties and tasks complete, open Task Verification and choose Workshop (facilitated) mode.

2

Click 🔒 Finalise & create live voting session. This locks the duties and tasks and issues a session ID and participant link.

3

Share the link or display the QR code on the projector. Participants open it and rate.

🔄

Collecting and closing

4

Click 🔄 Refresh voting results to pull the latest votes. Means and priority indices update on the dashboard.

5

When the panel is done, click 🔒 Close voting to stop further submissions.

6

Export the results as JSON, CSV, or a verification report in PDF or Word.

✅
Each project keeps its own live session. Switching projects in the sidebar shows that project’s link, QR code and results — sessions never bleed into one another.
⚠️
Live voting needs an internet connection on the facilitator’s device and on the participants’. Individual rating mode works fully offline.
🧩 Clusters

Competency Clusters tab

Group related tasks into competency clusters, then define the range of variables and the performance criteria for each.

1

From Task Verification, click ⚡ Proceed to competency clustering. You may also proceed without verifying.

2

Tasks appear in the Available Tasks pool, highest priority index first.

3

Select tasks and click Create cluster, or let the AI assistant suggest groupings across duties by shared purpose, process or skill.

4

Write the performance criteria — what competent performance looks like, in observable and measurable terms.

5

Add the range of variables — the conditions, contexts and materials across which the competency must hold.

💡
Performance criteria are the bridge between the chart and the curriculum. Each one should be specific, observable and measurable — they become the raw material for the Learning Outcomes tab, and a vague criterion produces a vague outcome.
🎓 Outcomes

Learning Outcomes tab

Turn performance criteria into learning outcomes — the point where occupational analysis becomes training design.

Pattern How it maps When to use it
A — One-to-oneOne learning outcome per performance criterion.Fine-grained assessment; strong traceability from criterion to outcome.
B — Many-to-oneSeveral related criteria combine into one broader outcome.Shorter outcome lists; suits modular or credit-based structures.
C — HybridMixes both, choosing per cluster.Real curricula rarely fit one shape throughout.
☑️
Generate from a selectionTick specific criteria to generate from just those. With none ticked, every unused criterion is used.
➕
Added, never replacedGenerated outcomes join the list; anything you wrote by hand stays exactly as it was.
🔗
Traceability preservedEach outcome records which criteria it addresses, so the line back to the original task never breaks.
📋
Evidence requirementsState what evidence demonstrates achievement — the basis of the assessment plan.
📦 Modules

Module Mapping tab

Organise learning outcomes into training modules — the last step before curriculum writing begins.

📦

Building modulesBy hand, or in one click

1

Select outcomes from the available list and click ✨ Create new module, then name it.

2

Or use ⚡ One module per outcome — instant, offline, and a reasonable starting skeleton.

3

Or use 🤖 AI group & sequence — it groups related outcomes, names each module, and orders them foundational to advanced.

4

Set duration, credit hours and prerequisites for each module.

5

Export the mapping as JSON for the Module Builder tool, or include it in the PDF and Word exports.

🔁
Both automatic options replace the existing modules — your learning outcomes are never touched. If you regenerate outcomes upstream, use 🔁 Regenerate from here on this tab rather than rebuilding by hand.

📊 Skills Matrix

Skills Level Matrix

Map generic workplace competencies onto four qualification levels — a skills framework embedded directly in the chart.

Level Description
CraftsmanThe top level — fully independent, and able to supervise and train others.
SkilledWorks independently with minimal supervision.
Semi-skilledNeeds guidance on the more complex tasks.
FoundationEntry level — requires close supervision and instruction.

Categories include communication, teamwork, self-marketing, problem solving, entrepreneurship, computer and ICT skills, foreign languages and mathematical skills — plus any category you add yourself.

🌐
The matrix is written in the interface language at the moment you create it, and it stays in that language afterwards. It is content, not interface — switching the interface language later does not rewrite text you may have edited.

📄

Arabic in exported documentsWhat to expect, and what to check

PDF. Arabic letters are shaped and joined correctly, the text is reordered for right-to-left reading, and the Arabic font is embedded inside the file — so the document looks the same on a colleague’s machine, a printer, or a phone, whether or not that device has the font installed.

Word. Arabic runs are marked as Arabic text with right-to-left direction, tables are mirrored, and the document font covers the Arabic range — so Word applies its own Arabic typography instead of treating the text as unknown Latin.

File names. Arabic project names survive into the downloaded file name instead of being stripped away.

🌐
This guide has its own switcher at the top of the page, and it opens in whatever language you were using in the tool. You can also link to a language directly by adding ?lang=ar, ?lang=fr or ?lang=en to the address.

📴 Offline

Offline use and updatesNew

Training rooms lose connectivity. The tool is built to keep working when that happens.

📲
Install itUse your browser’s Install or Add to home screen option. It then opens like any other app, from its own icon.
✅
What works offlineEvery tab, every export, the Arabic font, individual rating, and this guide if you opened it once while online.
🚫
What needs the networkAI generation of any kind, and live workshop voting. Both fail cleanly and tell you why.
🔔
Update bannerWhen a new version is published, a small banner offers to update. Nothing changes until you accept.
📶
Before a workshop with poor connectivity: open the tool online once so everything is cached, generate whatever drafts you need in advance, and export a JSON backup. You can then run the whole session offline and collect ratings individually.
💡 Tips

Tips and best practices

📋
Chart Info first, alwaysOccupation title and scope of work set the boundary for every generated stage. Ten minutes here saves an hour of correction later.
✨
Draft before the workshop, not instead of itBring a generated draft into the room as a provocation for the panel to argue with. The authority of the chart comes from the practitioners.
📸
Snapshot before generatingAny generation that replaces content deserves a named checkpoint first. It is faster than undo and survives a reload.
💾
Export after every phaseExport the JSON after duties, after verification, after clustering. The file name carries date and time, so the files version themselves.
🃏
Card view in the roomIt mirrors the sticky-card wall the panel already knows, which keeps the discussion on the content rather than on the software.
📊
Table view for bulk workSwitch to it when pasting from a spreadsheet or restructuring many tasks at once.
🔢
Pick the formula deliberatelyI×F for standard priority. I×F×D when you need to estimate training load and sequence the curriculum.
📡
Rehearse the live sessionRun a two-minute test with a colleague on the venue’s network before the panel arrives.
📁
One project per occupationName projects meaningfully — the name becomes the export file name and the label you search by.
↩
Undo covers structureAdding and removing duties and tasks is undoable. Fast typing inside a card collapses into a single undo step.
